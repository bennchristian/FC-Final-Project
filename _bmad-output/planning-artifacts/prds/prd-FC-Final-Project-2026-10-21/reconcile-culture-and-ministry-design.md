# Reconcile: *Culture and Ministry Design*

**How to find things again.** `p.` is the page number printed in the document. `L` is the line
number in the `.md` file. Section headings are given exactly as the document prints them.
Descriptions are paraphrased so the source is not reproduced. Named terms such as "posture of
humility" are the document's own labels.

## 1. What the document is

- **Title and series.** *Culture and Ministry Design*, from a course branded **IMMERSION**. It is
  56 pages, converted from a PDF, so there are OCR fragments and picture-text noise (for example
  L5-21 and L639).
- **Author and publisher.** Not stated anywhere. It is a church-planter training workbook. It
  refers to pausing "the video" (L1138), to a separate "five-chapter gospel" lesson (L672, L704,
  L1092, L1110), and to "a different module" on missional small groups (L977). So it is one part
  of a larger video curriculum.
- **Audience and setting.** Church planters and leaders in **India**: Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi,
  Cochin, and Gujarati, Telugu, Hindi and Marathi cultures. The outsiders in its examples are
  mostly Hindu or secular. Its frameworks transfer to other settings; its examples do not.
- **Sources it draws on.** Tim Keller, *Center Church* (the four-postures matrix). Dean Flemming,
  *Contextualization in the New Testament* (spelled both "Fleming" and "Flemming"). Michael
  Pocock. Francis Clooney on Roberto de Nobili. Scott Sunquist. Alan Hirsch. James K.A. Smith.
  Tyler Prieb. Sam Storms on common grace. IDEO, Stanford and the Interaction Design Foundation
  on design thinking.
- **Purpose.** To teach planters how they are postured toward culture, how to contextualize the
  gospel without diluting it, how to build a sending ("missional") church, and how to design
  ministry iteratively.
- **Structure.** Four modules. Each follows the same pattern: Definitions, Tensions (two failure
  extremes), Biblical Foundations, Frame (the model), Exercise, Case Study, Summary. Reflection
  questions appear throughout.

| Module | Pages | Lines | Core frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cultural Engagement | 8-21 | 103-466 | Artifacts, narratives and longings; Keller's four postures |
| 2. Contextualization | 22-35 | 472-817 | Over- versus under-contextualization; Gather, Study, Design |
| 3. Missional Church | 36-44 | 821-1050 | Attractional versus sending church; the Four Fields |
| 4. Gospel-shaped Design Thinking | 45-56 | 1054-1339 | Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test |

**What it is not.** It says nothing about social media or digital communication; the word
"media" appears only twice, in passing. It has **no typology of non-believers or outsiders**.
The typologies it does have describe *Christians and churches*: postures, traditionalists and
transformers, attractional and sending churches. The PRD has to adapt these frameworks; it cannot
lift them as they are.

## 2. Frameworks, models, typologies and principles

### 2a. Defining tiers or types of outsider persona

The document has no persona model. These are the closest building blocks.

### 2b. Criteria for good witness and cultural appropriateness

| Framework | Where | Rubric use |
|---|---|---|
| **The gospel lens** affirms what is biblical in a culture and rejects what is idolatrous | INTRODUCTION TO CULTURAL ENGAGEMENT, p.9, L155 | Does the post affirm something good in its audience's world, or only criticise? (See §4: the "rejects idolatry" half is a theological verdict.) |
| **Salt and light**: salt is valuable, preserves only when it is in contact with food, and keeps its distinct flavour; light shows good deeds in ordinary places (workplaces, cafes) | BIBLICAL FOUNDATION: SALT AND LIGHT, p.12-13, L222-248 | Three checks: engaged with ordinary life rather than sealed off; still distinctive; shows **word and deed**, not only claims. |
| **Two failure extremes**: *withdrawing and isolationism* versus *compromising and syncretism*; and *passive* versus *active influence* | TENSIONS PT. 1, p.14, L263-279; TENSIONS PT. 2, p.15, L287-299 | The rubric should flag **both** ditches: insular, church-only content, and content that cannot be told apart from the surrounding culture. |
| **Keller's four postures**, each with the extreme it can slide into. **Humility** (excellent work done humbly) can slide into a *sacred-secular divide*. **Common good** can slide into *syncretism*. **Distinctive worldview** can slide into *triumphalism and ungodly nationalism*. **Counterculture** can slide into *demonization*. Triumphalism and nationalism fit the political content in Bennett's brain dump. Demonization fits the content that is offensive on purpose. |
| **Contextualization**: deliberately crossing cultural barriers to translate, adapt and embody the gospel without diluting it. Includes the warning about insider jargon. | DEFINITIONS: CONTEXTUALIZATION, p.23, L488-496; Module 2 SUMMARY, L809 | A rubric dimension for jargon and accessibility. It matches the brain dump's point about content that is "unaware" (jargon, insider experiences treated as normal). |
| **Over- versus under-contextualization**: *transformers* over-contextualize and make an idol of the current culture; *traditionalists* under-contextualize and make an idol of the culture they came from | TENSIONS: OVER-CONTEXTUALIZATION V. (5) Gives a clear, definite answer. | BIBLICAL FOUNDATIONS PT. 3: PAUL IN ATHENS, p.28, L612-626 | **The best ready-made skeleton for rubric v1**: five criteria from a source the cohort will recognise. | **Five-chapter gospel study questions**: Creation (affirm what is good), Fall (critique idols lovingly, gently and precisely), Redemption (model Jesus), Renewal (serve), Restoration (envision a future with God) | FRAME PT. 2: STUDYING INFORMATION, p.31, L704-714 | Another rubric, or a structure for outreach plans. ## 4. Conflicts with the planned rules, and how the document sharpens them

### Personas are believable people, not caricatures

**Mostly reinforced, with one trap.**

- **Reinforced.** De Nobili's respect for Hindu intellect (p.25). The atheist whose protest
  against tyranny is affirmed (p.9). Paul refuses to condemn the Athenians (p.28). Jethro treats
  Moses as a person, not a project (p.49). Acts 15 deals with people, not problems (p.51). Read
  what outsiders make, not what Christians write about them (p.30).
- **Sharpened.** Build each persona from the three dimensions of culture plus a named sub-culture
  plus demographic fields (§2a). Include people of other faiths and church-hurt dechurched people,
  not only secular sceptics.
- **The trap.** The document itself sometimes slips into the moves personas must avoid.
  - **It diagnoses outsiders' hearts.** In its example, people work 70-80 hours *because* they
    idolise their careers (p.31-32, L725-736).
  - **It stereotypes.** It describes "stereotypically millennial" young adults who live for
    experiences (p.54, L1287).
  - **It frames longings as gaps to fill.** It treats a culture's longings as something to
    redirect to Jesus (p.11, L195).

  A persona built straight from this material becomes a target profile with a hole to fill. The
  PRD rule should say that **a persona reports what the person notices and feels, in their own
  terms. It never reports the tool's theological diagnosis of their motives.**

### Feedback is charitable and specific, with no theological verdicts

**Partly in conflict. This is the main thing to reconcile.**

- **The conflict.** The document is openly theological and evaluative. The gospel lens rejects
  what is idolatrous (p.9). The Fall question asks for a critique of a culture's idols (p.31).
  Paul refuses to compromise truth claims (p.28). The posture extremes carry verdict-laden names
  (p.17-18). A rubric lifted directly from it would say things like "this is syncretism" or
  "this is not the gospel".
- **The resolution the document itself makes possible.**
  1. **Score how a post comes across, not whether it is right.** Measure communication, posture
     and accessibility as outsiders would experience them.
  2. **Use the "offense of the gospel" distinction without ruling on it.** The tool can report
     that a persona was put off by X and that the reaction came from the claim, from the tone, or
     from jargon. It does **not** rule whether that offense was the gospel's or was needless. That
     is the ministry's **discernment** (p.18), and it is the document's own key word.
  3. **Critique forms, not DNA** (p.47). Format, wording, tone and audience assumptions are open
     to feedback. Doctrinal convictions are not.
- **Charitable is reinforced.** Affirm before critiquing (the Creation question comes first,
  p.31). Do not condemn (p.28). Apply the five willingnesses (p.21).
- **Specific is reinforced.** Gather, Study, Design ties each recommendation to an observation
  (p.31-32). Jethro names the problem *and the reason for it* (p.49).

### Reactions are labelled simulated

**Not addressed directly, since the document predates AI tools, but strongly supported.**

- The document insists that understanding outsiders comes from **time spent listening to real
  people** (p.29, p.35 L817, p.53). In design-thinking terms, a simulated reaction is not
  **Empathize**. It is at best an **Ideate** or **Prototype** artifact. The interviews with real
  outsiders are the **Test**.
- **Sharpened rule.** Label every simulated reaction as a hypothesis, and pair each persona with
  interview questions that would confirm or overturn it. Where a persona draws on demographic
  data, cite the basis (the Gathering stage, p.29).

## 5. Top 5 takeaways for the PRD

1. **Build rubric v1 on the five Paul-in-Athens marks** (p.28): cultural awareness, convictions
   held clearly, no condemnation, dialogue and questions invited, a clear answer given. **Add a
   jargon and accessibility dimension** (p.23; item 10 on p.43). Never treat "a persona was
   offended" as automatically bad witness. Report where the offense came from and leave the
   verdict to the ministry.
2. **The document gives no outsider tiers; Bennett must define them.** A good candidate axis is
   Paul's three audiences, ordered by how much religious vocabulary the outsider shares:
   synagogue (dechurched or nominal), Lystra (another faith or spirituality), Athens (secular and
   sceptical) (p.32-33). **Build each persona from cultural artifacts, narratives and longings
   plus a named sub-culture** (p.10-12). Include church-hurt and other-faith outsiders.
3. **Use Keller's four postures and their extremes as the whole-profile lens** (p.17-18). Every
   posture is legitimate; failure is sliding into its extreme (sacred-secular divide, syncretism,
   triumphalism and nationalism, demonization). This fits the political and offensive accounts in
   the brain dump. Add the **attractional** pattern (p.38) for feeds that are all invitations.
4. **Shape outreach plans as Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test, delivered the way Jethro
   gave his advice** (p.49-54): ask, name the problem and its reason, propose one small post
   experiment, then re-score. **Critique forms, not DNA** (p.47). The loop also justifies the
   versioned rubric.
5. **Interview plans listen first and never argue** (p.29). Adapt the five neighbourhood questions
   (p.30) and the affirm-then-broken questions (p.53). Present simulated reactions as hypotheses
   for interviews to test. The document's view that contextualization comes from time with real
   people (p.35) is the PRD's case for both the interview plans and the "simulated" label.
