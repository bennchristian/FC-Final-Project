# Interview Plans

These plans are for the interviews Bennett runs himself. They are research done outside the product
([`PRD.md`](../PRD.md) §5, OQ-3), and they are how the Personas and the Rubric get grounded in real
people (UJ-4, SM-4). What people say here drives new Persona Versions and Rubric Versions (FR-20,
FR-21). Simulated reactions are hypotheses, and these interviews test them.

- **Plan 1, non-believers.** Do the Persona Reactions ring true? Do the Criteria capture what
  actually puts people off or draws them in?
- **Plan 2, budding Christian influencers and ministries.** Would the Evaluations, the Gentler
  Rewrite and the Outreach Message be useful, and would they be received well?

Not covered: leaders and the Discussion Questions (UJ-1, FR-12).

## 0. Common to both plans

### 0.1 Codes

Criterion numbers follow [`PRD.md`](../PRD.md) §4.2. The research digest numbers its criteria
differently, so do not use its numbers here.

| Code | Criterion | Code | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Outsider Clarity | C6 | Humility & Integrity |
| C2 | Cultural Awareness | C7 | Love Made Visible |
| C3 | Gentleness & Respect | C8 | Invitation & Dialogue |
| C4 | Welcome over Judgment | C9 | Clear Conviction |
| C5 | Kingdom over Party | C10 | Self-Presentation Alignment (own content only) |

Personas are named in full. **Persona set** means the set as a whole: who is missing, who
overlaps. **Offence Source** means the FR-4 rule that offence from tone or jargon lowers a score,
while offence from the claim itself is reported but does not lower it.

### 0.2 Listening posture: listen, never argue

This is the rule for every interview. *Culture and Ministry Design* (p.29) says to listen first and
never argue at the information-gathering stage.

1. **No arguing, correcting, defending or evangelising.** Do not defend the church, the faith, the
   tool or the posts. If they say something you think is wrong, write it down, because it is data.
2. **Get their reaction before you show the tool's.** Never show a simulated reaction or a score
   until you have written down their own reaction to the same post. If you show the tool first, you
   learn whether they agree with the AI, not whether the AI agrees with them.
3. **Ask what is good first, then what is broken** (*Culture and Ministry Design* p.53).
4. **Ask for stories, not opinions.** Use questions like "Can you think of a time…?", "What
   happened next?", "What makes you say that?" and "Tell me more."
5. **Don't lead.** Don't name a Criterion or a Persona before they do, and don't ask "Wouldn't you
   say…?". Respond to everything with the same neutral thanks, never with "Great!" and never with a
   sceptical "Hmm."
6. **Leave silences.** The second thing someone says is often the true one.
7. **If they ask what you believe,** answer briefly and honestly, then offer to talk more after the
   interview. That conversation is not part of the record.
8. **If something painful comes up,** thank them. Don't probe for detail, offer to pause or skip,
   and record only what they are comfortable with. You are not their counsellor in this
   conversation.
9. **Keep the five willingnesses** (*Culture and Ministry Design* p.21): to listen, to ask gently
   and kindly, to change your mind, to hold your convictions without needing to defend them here,
   and to let go of pride.

### 0.3 Keep real answers apart from simulated output

This repository is **public on GitHub**, so interview records, names and the code key never go in
it. Keep them in a private folder outside the repo, such as a personal cloud-drive folder.

- **One record sheet per interview.** Use the code N-01, N-02… for non-believers and C-01,
  C-02… for creators and ministries. If you keep a key from codes to names, keep it in a separate
  private file. You need it only to delete someone's notes on request.
- **The sheet holds only what the person said,** with your own reading of it in a separate column
  marked as yours.
- **Never paste simulated Persona Reactions, Rubric Scores or rewrites into the sheet.** Refer to
  them by Review and version instead, so that AI text can never later be mistaken for a human quote
  (PRD §7.3).
- **Write the sheet up within 24 hours.** If you recorded audio, delete it once the sheet is done.
- **Remove names and identifying details before using an AI assistant** to sort notes. The consent
  wording promises this.

**Record sheet** (copy one per interview):

```markdown
# N-03 · 2026-11-02 · Plan 1
Consent: verbal yes · quotes OK: yes/no · audio: yes/no (deleted on: ____)
Nearest Persona, in their own words (Q11): ...
Shown: posts P1, P4, P6 · tool output from Review <id>, witness-rubric@1, persona-set@1

| Q | Tag | What they said ("verbatim" or close paraphrase) | My reading (mine, not theirs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Seeker | "..." | |

Simulated vs real (Q9): Persona ______ · rings true / partly / off · what was off: ...
What surprised me: ...
Possible changes (one line each, tagged): ...
```

### 0.4 From answers to new versions

- **One interview is a story, and two pointing the same way are a pattern.** Change a Criterion or
  a Persona card when at least two independent interviews point the same way. The exception is a
  clear caricature (a "do not portray as" breach in §1.4): fix that after one.
- **A Persona change** creates a new Persona Version. **A Criterion change**, whether a rewording,
  an addition or a removal, creates a new Rubric Version. Scores are never compared across Rubric
  Versions (FR-4).
- **Each version's change note cites interview codes and counts, never names,** for example
  "persona-set@2: Drifter card revised after N-02 and N-05." Record on each Persona card how many
  interviews it now rests on (research §5.2).
- **After publishing,** re-run the interview post set (§0.5) on the new version, and check that the
  output moved the way the interviews said it should.
- **These are grounding stories, not statistics.** The sample is small and drawn from people you
  know, so never claim that "most non-believers" think anything.
- **Some decisions are deliberate,** such as the one-sided political lean (PRD §4.3) and the rule
  that offence from the claim does not lower a score (FR-4). Record any evidence against them in the
  findings log, but change them only through a PRD decision.

### 0.5 The post set (Plan 1)

Before the first non-believer interview, choose six posts. Use the same set in every interview so
that answers can be compared, and show each person three of them, rotating through the six. Cover:

| Post | Kind | Mainly tests |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Heavy with church vocabulary or bare verse references | C1 |
| P2 | Fuses faith with party or nation. Alternate left-coded and right-coded posts across interviews. | C5 |
| P3 | A hard conviction stated gently | C9, Offence Source |
| P4 | Combative "speak the truth" | C3, C4, Emboldened Believer |
| P5 | Lands well: the tool calls it fine | FR-5 |
| P6 | Polished ministry self-promotion, or love shown in action | C6, C7 |

- Use **fictional posts** written in the style of real ones, or **real public posts** with names,
  handles, faces and comments blurred (PRD §7.2).
- Run every post through the tool beforehand, and note the Review and the versions. Print or
  screenshot the Persona Reaction and Gentler Rewrite you will show, on paper or a screen separate
  from the record sheet.
- The set becomes part of the calibration set and is re-run on every new version (§0.4; research
  §3.5).

## 1. Plan 1: Non-believers

**Goal:** test SM-4, whether real non-believers recognise their Persona's reactions as plausible, and
test whether the Criteria capture what actually puts them off or draws them in.

### 1.1 Recruiting

- **Adults only (18+).** The PRD's Seeker is a peer of Emily's school friends (§4.3, UJ-1), so
  possibly a teenager. Interviewing minors needs a parent's or guardian's consent and more care, so
  for now adults aged 18–24 stand in for the Seeker.
- **First round: six people,** one nearest each non-believer Persona: Seeker, Drifter, Neighbor of
  Another Faith, Exvangelical, Skeptic and Progressive. The Emboldened Believer is a Christian, so
  Plan 2 tests that Persona.
- **Second round: a second person for each Persona, to reach the SM-4 bar.** Start with Seekers
  and Drifters, who stand for far more people, and add any further interviews there before adding
  more Exvangelicals, who are few but loud online (research §1.3).
- **SM-4 bar:** at least two interviews per non-believer Persona, twelve in all. A Persona passes
  when most of its interviewees mark Q9 "rings true" or "partly". The PRD's SM-4 has no count yet,
  so adding this bar to it is a separate change.
- **Don't label people when inviting them.** Guess the nearest Persona from what you know. They
  will place themselves in Q11, and if your guess was wrong, record their placement.
- **Look beyond close friends.** People who know you are a Christian may soften what they say, so
  include friends of friends, colleagues and neighbours. Tell them plainly that a critical answer
  is more useful than a kind one.
- **Meet where they are comfortable,** such as a café, their place or a call. Never meet in a
  church building (*Culture and Ministry Design* p.29).
- **Allow 45–60 minutes.** If you can, thank them with a coffee or a small gift.

**Invitation** (adapt to your own words):

> I'm building a tool for a course that helps Christians see how their social-media posts come
> across to people who don't share their faith. It uses AI to guess how different people might
> react, and I want to check those guesses against real people. Would you give me 45 minutes to
> look at a few posts and tell me honestly what you think? I won't try to change your mind.

### 1.2 Consent

Read this aloud at the start, or send it ahead and confirm it at the start. Record the answers on
the sheet.

> Thanks for doing this. I'm building a tool for my Frontier Commons course that helps Christians
> see how their social-media posts might come across to people who don't share their faith. It uses
> AI to imagine how different kinds of people might react. I want to check those guesses against
> real people, and you're one of them.
>
> - This is a conversation of about 45 minutes. I'll show you a few posts and ask what you think.
> - There are no right answers. I'm not here to argue about faith or change your mind. Your honest
>   reaction is what helps, especially the critical parts.
> - You can skip any question or stop at any time.
> - I'll take notes. With your OK I'll also record audio, only to check my notes, and delete it
>   once they're written up. **(Audio OK?)**
> - Your name won't appear in my notes or anywhere in the project; I'll use a code. My notes stay
>   private and are not stored with the project's code.
> - What you say will change how the tool imagines people's reactions and what it checks posts
>   for. With your OK I may quote you, without your name, in the tool's persona descriptions or
>   when I present the project. **(Quotes OK?)**
> - If I use an AI assistant to help sort my notes, I'll remove anything that identifies you first.
> - If you change your mind later, tell me and I'll delete your notes.
>
> Is all of that OK?

### 1.4 What each Persona assumes

These are the hypotheses Plan 1 tests, taken from PRD §4.3 and research §1.3. The right-hand
columns say what to listen for.

| Persona | The card assumes | It may be wrong if… | Do not portray as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seeker | Curious and open to Jesus, but lost in church language | they aren't curious, or it isn't the vocabulary that loses them | someone there only to be persuaded (`RULES.md`) |
| Drifter | Indifferent; scrolls past most religious content | they react strongly, or the indifference covers hurt | — |
| Neighbor of Another Faith | Respects faith; bristles at disparagement or pressure to convert | exclusive claims don't bother them, but something else does, such as ignorance of their tradition | a foreign or threatening other (research §5.1) |
| Exvangelical | Knows the faith, was hurt by church, still admires Jesus | they don't admire Jesus, or they're done rather than hurt | always traumatised or angry (research §5.2) |
| Skeptic | Atheist, critical but fair-minded; allergic to overclaiming and pressure | they're more hostile or more indifferent than the card | the militant atheist (research §5.2) |
| Progressive | A non-believer whose progressive convictions shape how posts land | their objection is to tone rather than teaching, or their politics doesn't drive it | a caricature of their politics (research §5.1) |

### 1.5 Questions

**Part A. Their experience** (no posts yet)

**Q1. "Where are you with faith or religion these days, and how did you get there?"**
- Follow-up: "Was church part of your life growing up?"
- Revises: **Persona set** and the nearest Persona's card (backstory, familiarity, church history,
  worldview).
- Listen for: someone who fits no Persona (a gap in the set) or fits two (an overlap), and details
  that contradict the card.

**Q2. "When posts from Christians or churches show up in your feed, what usually happens?"**
- Follow-up: "Which apps? Do you ever stop, read the comments, or reply?"
- Revises: the nearest Persona's platform habits and default Felt Response. Also tests the FR-17
  premise that a post lands on anyone's page.
- Listen for: how often they see such posts at all, and whether they scroll past or engage.

**Q3. "Think of a post by a Christian that put you off. What was it, and what about it got to you?"**
- Revises: **C3, C4, C5, C6** and **Offence Source**. If the answer fits no Criterion, it is a
  candidate for a new one.
- Listen for: which Criterion it maps to. Decide that afterwards, not during the interview. Note
  anything no Criterion would catch.

**Q4. "Now think of one that drew you in, or that you respected even if you didn't agree. What was different?"**
- Revises: **C2, C7, C8, C9** and the positive Felt Responses (FR-8).
- Listen for: whether the respect came from plain conviction (C9) or from gentleness, and whether
  their words match the tool's positive list: convicted, encouraged, uplifted, moved to openness.

**Part B. The posts** (three from the set, one at a time, with no tool output)

**Q5. "Imagine this came up while you were scrolling. What goes through your head? How does it leave you feeling?"**
- For P2, the political post, follow up with: "Does this change how you see Christians, or just
  this person?" (**C5**)
- Revises: the nearest Persona's Felt Response and reasons (FR-8), and the Felt Response vocabulary.
- Listen for: their own feeling words. Write them down verbatim, because they may not be words the
  tool uses.

**Q6. "Was there anything here you didn't follow, or that felt like it wasn't written for you?"**
- Revises: **C1** (its list of unexplained terms and bare verse references), **Seeker** and
  **Neighbor of Another Faith**.
- Listen for: the exact words they trip on. This is the most direct evidence for C1.

**Q7. "What exactly bothered you about it?"** (ask only if something did)
- Follow-up, if it isn't already clear: "Was it what they believe, the way they said it, or the
  words they used?"
- Revises: **Offence Source** and **C9**.
- Listen for: whether outsiders separate the claim from the tone at all, and whether a hard claim
  said gently (P3) still reads as respectful. If people consistently can't separate the two, the
  FR-4 rule needs a PRD decision, not a quiet change.

**Q8. "Does whoever posted this seem to understand people like you: your life, what you care about?"**
- Follow-up: "What would show you they did?"
- Revises: **C2**, and what the **Skeptic** and **Progressive** cards say each one values.

**Part C. The simulation** (tool output appears for the first time)

**Q9.** Show the simulated reaction of the Persona nearest to them, for a post they have already
reacted to. Say: "This is an AI's guess at how someone [the Persona's one line] might react."
**"What does it get right? What does it get wrong?"**
- Follow-up: "Would anyone you know react like this? Does it sound like a real person?"
- Revises: **that Persona's card** (voice, triggers, "do not portray as"). This question is the
  SM-4 measure.
- Record: rings true, partly, or off, plus what was off. Signs of caricature are a reaction that is
  too angry, too wounded or too easily persuaded (research §5.1).

**Q10.** Show the Gentler Rewrite next to the original. **"Which of these would you rather come
across in your feed? Does the second one still say the same thing?"**
- Follow-up: "Does anything in it feel fake or watered down?"
- Revises: **C9** (does the rewrite keep the conviction?), the FR-11 rewrite rules, and the
  Criteria that drove the rewrite.
- Listen for: "watered down" or "salesy". Either means the rewrite traded away conviction to sound
  gentle.

**Q11.** Show the seven Personas, one line each. **"The tool imagines these seven kinds of reader.
Is anyone here like you? Is anyone missing, or drawn unfairly?"**
- Follow-up, about the last one: "This is a Christian who takes a post as permission to be harsh.
  Have you seen that happen in the comments?" (**Emboldened Believer**)
- Revises: the **Persona set**: splits, merges, gaps, and each one-line description.
- Note: the set's one-sided political lean is deliberate (PRD §4.3). Record what they say about it
  in the findings log, but don't treat it as a mandate.

**Q12. "If a Christian friend asked you how to post so that people like you would actually listen, what would you tell them?"**
- Follow-up: show the list of Criteria C1–C9, one line each, and ask: "Is anything missing? Is
  anything on here that doesn't matter to you?"
- Revises: **the Rubric as a whole**: a new Criterion, or one to drop or merge. Also **C8**.

## 2. Plan 2: Budding Christian influencers and ministries

**Goal:** test whether the Evaluations, the Gentler Rewrite and the Outreach Message are useful
and received well, by showing creators and ministries what the tool says about their own posts.

### 2.1 Recruiting

- **Three to five participants.** Include at least two individual creators like Esther (UJ-2) and
  at least one ministry account, run by staff or a volunteer. Mix Instagram and TikTok.
- **Prefer people you don't supervise or mentor,** since they may find it hard to disagree with
  you. If you do supervise someone, say plainly that disagreeing is the point.
- **Timing.** The Demo Artifact can run these interviews from Fri 2026-10-23, but only as an
  Others' Review, so C10 shows N/A. On the Web App (week of 2026-10-26), use an Own-Content Review
  so that the Creator Foundation and C10 are tested too. Prefer the Web App if you can wait.
- **Afterwards,** you may invite them to use the Web App themselves. That counts toward SM-3 if
  they do. Make it an invitation, not an ask.
- **Allow 45–60 minutes.**

**Invitation:**

> I'm building a tool that shows Christians how their posts might come across to people who don't
> share their faith. Would you let me run three to five of your own public posts through it, then
> spend 45 minutes telling me whether what it says is useful, fair, or off? It's the tool being
> tested, not you.

### 2.2 Consent

Send this ahead with the request for posts, and confirm it at the start.

> Thanks for doing this. I'm building a tool for my Frontier Commons course that helps Christians
> see how their social-media posts might come across to people who don't share their faith.
>
> - You choose three to five of your own public posts. One you're proud of and one you're unsure
>   about is ideal, and a draft is welcome too.
> - To review them, the tool sends your posts to Claude, an AI service made by Anthropic.
> - The tool reviews posts, not people. It does not judge your faith or your theology. Its
>   "reactions" are AI guesses about how different kinds of people might respond, not real
>   people's views.
> - It's the tool being tested, not you. If you think it's wrong, that's exactly what I need to
>   hear.
> - Nothing is posted or sent to anyone, and I won't show the results or your posts to anyone
>   without asking you first.
> - The conversation takes about 45 minutes. You can skip any question or stop at any time.
> - I'll take notes. With your OK I'll also record audio, only to check my notes, and delete it
>   once they're written up. **(Audio OK?)**
> - Your name and handle won't appear in my notes or anywhere in the project; I'll use a code. With
>   your OK I may quote you without your name when I present the project. **(Quotes OK?)**
> - If I use an AI assistant to help sort my notes, I'll remove anything that identifies you first.
> - If you change your mind later, tell me and I'll delete your notes, and the review of your posts
>   too.
>
> Is all of that OK?

### 2.3 Before the interview

- Run their posts through the tool as one Review, and note the Review and the versions.
  - On the Web App, first set up their Creator Foundation with them by message: how they want to
    come across, and their Target Audiences ranked.
  - Generate a Ministry Outreach Plan for the same Review.
- Don't send them the output in advance. Their first reaction, in the room, is the data. Do tell
  them what kinds of output they will see.
- Delete the Review when they ask, or when the project ends.

| A. Their account | 10 | Q1–Q3. No tool output yet. |

### 2.5 Questions

**Part A. Their account** (no tool output yet)

**Q1. "Tell me about your account. Who are you hoping it reaches, and how do you want to come across?"**
- Follow-ups: "If you had to rank who you post for, who comes first?" and "If you mostly post for
  Christians, does it matter to you how someone outside the faith reads it?"
- Revises: **C10**, the Creator Foundation and its list of Target Audiences (FR-15), the Persona
  order (FR-10, PRD OQ-6), and the "lands anywhere" reminder (FR-17).

**Q2. "How do you decide a post is ready to go up? Does anyone see it first?"**
- Revises: whether the Draft Checkpoint (FR-16) fits how they actually work.
- Listen for: who already gives them feedback, and when. The tool has to fit into that.

**Q3. "When you picture someone who doesn't share your faith seeing your posts, who do you picture?"**
- Revises: the **Persona set**: which Personas creators already have in mind and which they never
  picture. Also FR-10 order.

**Part B. The Evaluation** (their own posts, one at a time)

**Q4. "Here's what the tool made of this post. What's useful? What's confusing?"**
- Follow-up: "Is anything you care about missing from what it checks?"
- Revises: the names and one-line definitions of whichever Criteria **C1–C9** their posts engage,
  the Evidence-and-reason format (FR-4), and the 1–5 scale. Also **C2**.
- Listen for: Criteria they can't read without your help. Their wording needs work.

**Q5. "Is there a score you'd push back on? Tell me why."**
- Revises: **that Criterion**, **Offence Source**, and FR-5 (a post called fine that they think has
  problems, or the reverse).
- Listen for: disagreement. It is the most valuable answer in Plan 2, so don't explain the score.

**Q6. "Read the simulated reactions. Do any of them change how you see the post? Do any seem unfair, to you or to the kind of person they're meant to be?"**
- Revises: **each Persona's card**, and the "simulated" label (FR-9).
- Listen for: talk of the reactions as if they were real people. If they do that, the label isn't
  working.

**Q7. "This reader is a Christian who might take a post as permission to be harsh. Does that match anything you've seen in your comments or among your followers?"**
- Revises: the **Emboldened Believer** card, and **C3**.
- Listen for: which kinds of post stir that harshness. That is the trigger list for the card.

**Q8. "Is there anything flagged here that you'd say anyway, knowing some people will be offended?"**
- Revises: **C9** and **Offence Source**.
- Listen for: how they tell the offence of the message from the offence of the manner, and whether
  the tool's Offence Source label matched their view.

**Part C. The rewrite and the outreach message**

**Q9.** Show the Gentler Rewrite next to their original. **"Is this still your message? Would you
post it, or something like it? What would you change?"**
- Revises: the FR-11 rewrite rules (keep the main message), **C9**, and **C10** (does it sound like
  them?).
- Listen for: "doesn't sound like me", which is a problem of voice, versus "doesn't say what I
  meant", which means the message was lost. They need different fixes.

**Q10.** Show the Outreach Message. **"Imagine a friend sent you this about your account. How
would it land? What would make you take it seriously, and what would make you defensive?"**
- Follow-up: "Would it matter who sent it: a friend, a leader, someone you don't know?"
- Revises: the Outreach Message rules (FR-13: affirmation first, at most two or three concerns,
  concrete changes), the Introducer journey (UJ-3), and which Criteria become Talking Points.
- Listen for: whether the affirmation reads as genuine or as a set-up, and whether two or three
  concerns are already too many.

**Q11.** Show the list of Criteria C1–C10, one line each. **"What does good witness online look
like to you? Is anything missing from this list, or on it that shouldn't be?"**
- Revises: **the Rubric as a whole**: a new Criterion, or one to drop or merge. Also **C2**.

**Q12. "Would you use something like this, on your own posts or before you post? What would stop you?"**
- Revises: the Own-Content Review and Draft Checkpoint (FR-16), and SM-3.
- Listen for: what stops them, whether time, trust, cost or tone. These are the barriers to real
  use.

### 2.6 Listening notes for Plan 2

- **Don't defend or explain a score** until they have finished. Their disagreement is the finding.
- **Don't add your own critique** of their posts, and don't side with the tool to be helpful.
- **If they move into whether a belief is true or sound,** note it and move on. The tool does not
  rule on doctrine (PRD §5), and neither does the interview.
