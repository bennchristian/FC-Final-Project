# Living Letter — 2-minute video: script and cues

**Status:** draft 1 (2026-10-29). For Bennett to edit before recording.
**Structure:** the five beats of the demo-day story guide: Hook (0:00–0:15) · The person and the problem
(0:15–0:35) · Show it working (0:35–1:30) · What users taught you (1:30–1:45) · Where it goes next
(1:45–2:00). Hard limit: **2:00**.
**Format:** the same as the 3-minute video (`docs/video/script.md`): motion design over the live Artifact,
with Bennett's voice-over recorded first. The VO is the clock: every cue fires on a spoken word.
**Demo Review:** the same Post as before and as "Try the demo" (FR-24): `artifact/sample-review.json`, the
public reel "Should a Christian attend a gay wedding?" (`witness-rubric@2 · persona-set@1 · prompts@2`).
Every quote below is word for word from that Review.

## How to read this

Read the **VO** column aloud. **Bold words** are suggested cue triggers. The **Cue** column is a suggestion
(*idea:*) to keep, change or delete; write the final one as `on "<trigger words>" → <what happens>`.
**[pause ~Ns]** means leave silence while a quote is on screen to be read. **[Bennett: …]** is a line only
you can write.

Motion vocabulary as before: **write-on**, **pop**, **zoom in / out**, **highlight**, **pan**, **cut**, **hold**.

## 1. Hook (0:00–0:15)

| # | VO | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | A Christian creator posted this reel to answer, **"Should a Christian attend a gay wedding?"** | *idea:* blank screen. The question writes on in the serif face, then the reel's own lines stack under it: "Of course not." · "Why are you gay?" · "throwing Bibles at them". |
| 1.2 | To people who **already agree**, it sounds **bold.** | *idea:* "bold" pops beside the lines. |
| 1.3 | But what does **everyone else** hear? | *idea:* the lines blur; hold on the question mark. Cut to the closed envelope. |

## 2. The person and the problem (0:15–0:35)

| # | VO | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Christian creators are posting **more than ever**, and Paul says we're letters **"known and read by everyone."** | *idea:* the envelope opens and the letter rises; highlight "known and read by everyone" and "2 Corinthians 3:2-3". |
| 2.2 | But the comments come from people who **already agree.** The people we most want to reach just **scroll past**, and think a little **less of Jesus.** | *idea:* zoom out; the letter sits small in the centre. "scroll past" writes on and slides off the edge. |

## 3. Show it working (0:35–1:30)

Three features, as a creator would use them: read a Post from its link, score it and see how it lands, then
fix it. The Review is replayed over the real Artifact as in the 3-minute video (see Production notes).

| # | VO | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Paste a link** to a reel or TikTok, and Living Letter **transcribes it.** Hit **Evaluate.** | *idea:* zoom into "2. Add Posts", "Link to the Post" chosen, link in the field. Cursor clicks **Add Post**, then **Evaluate Posts**; the progress state shows; results appear. Blur the link after the click. |
| 3.2 | Every post is scored on **ten Criteria of good witness**, from **Scripture**, **Paul in Athens**, and **research** on how outsiders see Christians. | *idea:* pan the score table. |
| 3.3 | This one scored **five out of five** for **conviction**, but **ones** on **gentleness**, **welcome** and **love.** | *idea:* highlight Clear Conviction 5, then sweep down Gentleness & Respect 1, Welcome over Judgment 1, Love Made Visible 1. The point: it is never marked down for what it believes. |
| 3.4 | Then **seven simulated people** react. The Seeker felt **pushed away.** [pause ~4s] | *idea:* pan to Persona Reactions; zoom on the Seeker; highlight "he doesn't see people like me or my friends at all". |
| 3.5 | And the one believer on the panel? **Licensed to mock.** [pause ~5s] | *idea:* zoom on the Emboldened Believer; highlight "shows me that mocking people is a fair way to make a point". |
| 3.6 | Then a **gentler rewrite** keeps the **same conviction**, said with kindness, | *idea:* zoom into the Gentler Rewrite; highlight "My answer is no, and I want to explain why as honestly as I can, whether you share my faith or not." |
| 3.7 | and **discussion questions** help a youth group talk it through. | *idea:* zoom into Discussion Questions; highlight "Imagine Jesus making a short video that answers 'Should a Christian attend a gay wedding?'" Hold. |

## 4. What users taught us (1:30–1:45)

The guide's line: "We assumed X. They said Y. We changed Z." This is the real change from version 1 to
version 2 of the rubric and prompts (live since 2026-10-23).

| # | VO | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | We **assumed** our personas sounded like **real people.** | *idea:* cut to plain cards over the Artifact, in the page's type: **We assumed** · **They said** · **We changed**. The first one fills in. |
| 4.2 | [Bennett: who] **testers said** they sounded like **editors**, and the scores ran **too kind.** | *idea:* the second card fills in. |
| 4.3 | So now personas **only react**, and **scoring got stricter.** Next, we'll check them against **interviews with real people** outside the faith. | *idea:* the third card fills in. Then click the **Roadmap** tab; highlight "New Rubric and Persona Versions". |

## Open decisions

- **The hook's quotes (1.1).** Opening on "Why are you gay?" is the strongest moment in the Review, and
  it is the reel's own words. If it feels too sharp for the room, open on the question alone.
- **Who the testers were (4.2).** The guide asks for real people. Name them ("our cohort", "three
  friends", etc.); if it was only your own test run, say "When I tested it, they sounded like editors."
- **Your why (2.3).** Only you can write it. Keep it to one sentence, or cut it to save ~5 seconds.
- **The closing line (5.2).** Kept from the 3-minute video. The guide wants one vision sentence plus one
  next step; this gives the next step ("Try the demo") and ends on the vision.
